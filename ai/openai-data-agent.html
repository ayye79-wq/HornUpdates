OpenAI's Data agent turns ChatGPT Work into an analytics layer

The new agent connects to company data, investigates business questions and can build interactive dashboards from a conversation.

By Horn Updates Editorial Desk · Updated September 11, 2026

OpenAI has introduced a Data agent inside ChatGPT Work, pushing its workplace product beyond document search and general-purpose assistance into business intelligence and analytics.

According to OpenAI, the agent can connect to approved enterprise sources including Amazon Redshift, Google BigQuery, Databricks, Snowflake, MongoDB and other systems. It can also bring documents from Google Drive and SharePoint into an analysis.

What changed

The important shift is not simply that ChatGPT can query a database. OpenAI is packaging the workflow around the question a business user actually asks: why did a metric move, which accounts are at risk, where is spending rising, and what should be investigated next?

The agent can follow those questions across multiple sources, show evidence behind its findings and convert the analysis into an interactive dashboard. OpenAI says it can also work with existing BI products including Power BI, Tableau, Sigma and ThoughtSpot.

Why it matters

This is another step in the move from standalone chatbots toward agents that operate across company systems. If these tools are reliable, some routine analytics work that currently requires SQL queries, dashboard requests or back-and-forth with a data team could move directly to business users.

That does not eliminate the need for data engineering. In fact, the quality of the result still depends heavily on clean source data, correct access controls, trusted metric definitions and good governance. OpenAI itself emphasizes semantic layers, organizational definitions and permissions as part of the setup.

What to watch

The biggest questions are accuracy, data permissions and how organizations audit the agent's work. A confident but incorrect explanation of a business metric can be more dangerous than no answer at all. Enterprises will also need to decide which employees can query which systems and how sensitive information is kept from crossing access boundaries.
